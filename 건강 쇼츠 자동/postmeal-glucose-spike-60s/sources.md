## Verified Claims Used

- CDC says regular physical activity helps manage blood sugar, and suggests starting with practical goals such as a 10-minute walk after dinner.
- CDC says common blood sugar check times can include before meals and two hours after meals, and lists a typical two-hour post-meal target of less than 180 mg/dL for many people with diabetes. Individual targets can differ.
- American Diabetes Association guidance notes that breaking up long sitting periods with brief activity every 30 minutes may be especially useful for people with type 2 diabetes.
- Diabetes UK explains that carbohydrate type and portion size affect how quickly blood sugar rises, and that protein, fat, and fiber can slow carbohydrate digestion and absorption. It also warns that GI is only one part of a balanced diet.

## Medical Safety Note

This video gives general health education, not diagnosis or personal treatment. People using insulin, sulfonylureas, or glucose-lowering medication should ask their care team how to exercise safely and what glucose targets are right for them.
